Ceiling Water Damage Repair: What Cheyenne Homeowners Should Know

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. Long before you see discoloration, the insulation is wet, moisture has followed the framing and a puddle sits on the hidden side of the panel. Common sources are the roof, an air conditioning drain, a pipe running overhead, or the bathroom one story up. Our team finds where it starts before any patching is done.

A coat of paint over the spot solves nothing. If the cavity above is still wet, the mark comes back and mold can start on the paper backing within 24 to 48 hours. The opening is kept as small as the damage allows, wet insulation comes out, joists and surrounding drywall are dried, and the area is patched, textured and painted to blend. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Stay out from under it and call.

Signs to Look For

  • Thin cracking in the ceiling finish centered on a wet looking area
  • Drip marks at the top of a wall where it meets the ceiling
  • Drywall tape lifting and seams or screw heads starting to show
  • Stale, mildew like odor you cannot trace to anything you can see
  • Patches of spotty discoloration that look like mold on the ceiling
  • Discolored rings overhead that get bigger each time it rains or the bathroom is used

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Asbestos in Older Ceilings

Some older popcorn textures and plasters contain asbestos. Have a sample tested before any scraping, cutting or removal if your ceiling is one of these.

Mold Above You

Even when the ceiling feels dry to the touch, the batts and the unseen face of the drywall remain damp, giving mold a hidden place to spread.

Water Travels Sideways

Water follows joists, pipes and ducts, so it can drip several feet from where it entered. Cutting in just under the mark may leave damp areas elsewhere untouched.

A Ceiling That Lets Go

Wet drywall is far heavier than dry and loses its grip on the fasteners. Whatever sits under a swollen area is at risk if that section comes down.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

Water runs along joists and soaks into insulation. The drywall absorbs it from the back, so the room below may still look fine.

24 to 48 hours

Stains and Bubbles

A stain forms and paint starts to blister. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Days 3 through 7

The Ceiling Bows

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. Between the joists the surface dips, joints split apart and a musty smell arrives below.

After a week

Sections May Fall

Saturated pieces may drop and carry soaked insulation down too. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.

Our Process

Our Ceiling Water Damage Repair Process

1

Find the Source

We track the water back to where it starts using moisture meters and thermal imaging, checking the room or attic above. Repairs are pointless until that leak is shut down.

2

Secure the Room

We cut power to any wet fixture and move or cover the furniture underneath. Where water is trapped overhead, it gets drained under control into containers.

3

Open and Dry

Wet insulation is removed along with any drywall that cannot stay. Drying equipment, meaning a dehumidifier and air movers, works on the framing and adjacent drywall, with antimicrobial treatment for water that was not clean.

4

Rebuild and Paint

After the meter shows normal levels, new insulation and drywall go up, joints are taped and finished, the texture is matched and a stain blocking primer goes on ahead of paint.
